# NOVIRA — Monetization Strategy Study

Companion to `Plan.md` (§5 Business model, §9 Marketing, §10 Budget) — that document already decided the *shape* of the revenue model (free Tier 1 → paid Tier 2 document review → paid Tier 3 apply-on-behalf → B2B placements as "the real long-term engine"). This document exists to pressure-test that shape against real comparable businesses (with a deliberate focus on India, since outbound labor/study migration from India to Germany is a large, mature, heavily-benchmarked corridor — much more so than Egypt's, which gives real numbers to anchor against) and against NOVIRA's actual current cost structure, then turn that into a concrete, sequenced answer to "how does this app make money without draining the founder's own pocket first."

Written 2026-09-11, prompted directly by the founder naming the real problem: hosting + AI costs are already being felt before any advertising spend has even started, and revenue thinking needs to catch up with build velocity rather than trail it indefinitely.

**Revised four times since**, each time documented in place rather than overwritten — see §4 for the current (2026-09-27) recommendation, and its own §4.6 for a summary of what the revisions before it said and why each was superseded. The throughline hasn't changed since the first revision: the founder correctly pushed back on leading with B2B — not technically/operationally ready to run a B2B pipeline yet, near-term priority is building a large user database and refining the app in Egypt, funded by user fees rather than profit-seeking B2B deals. §1–§3 (cost drivers, competitor research, original synthesis) are kept as-is below since they're still accurate background — §4 is where the plan actually lives now, and is the section to read first.

## 0. Opinion on doing this now, upfront

This is the right call, and the right moment for it — three reasons:

1. **You now have enough product to reason about concretely.** Six weeks ago "what does this cost per user" would have been a guess. Today you know exactly what a document verification call costs (a real Opus 5 vision call), what a University-research run costs (real enough that it burned a trial credit balance twice this session), and what the two AI-heavy surfaces in the app actually are. Grounding a monetization study in real, known cost drivers instead of hypothetical ones is only possible now.
2. **You caught this before ad spend, not after.** The worst version of this mistake is spending on Facebook/TikTok acquisition (§9) to bring in free-tier users against an AI cost structure that loses money per user — that compounds every week you don't notice it. Stopping to check the unit economics *before* turning on acquisition spend, while it's still zero, is exactly the right sequencing.
3. **It doesn't cost you the feature-building momentum you'd worry about losing.** Nothing below asks you to stop building match quality or opportunity data — it asks you to point the *next* increment of building at billing/paywall plumbing instead of another matching factor, once §4 settles what to build first. That's a redirection of the same breadth-first energy, not a pause on it.

The one caution: don't let this become a multi-week strategy exercise instead of a decision. §4 below converges on a fairly clear answer — read it, pick the near-term move, and get back to building against it. A study that takes longer to write than the feature it recommends is its own kind of cost leak.

## 1. NOVIRA's actual cost drivers today (the "deep look")

Reasoned from the real architecture (`CLAUDE.md`), not guessed. Where a dollar figure isn't something I have direct visibility into (I can't see your Azure/Anthropic billing dashboards), it's marked as an estimate with the reasoning shown — check the real numbers there before trusting these.

| Driver | What it is | Cost shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Document verification** (per upload) | One Claude Opus 5 vision call per document — image/PDF in, structured JSON out (identity fields, education fields, language-cert fields, concerns). | Variable, scales with usage. Rough estimate: a few thousand input tokens (image + prompt) + a few hundred–thousand output tokens (JSON + any adaptive thinking) → **roughly $0.03–$0.15 per document** at Opus 5 rates ($5/$25 per 1M tokens). At a 10-document-per-account cap (`DocumentsEndpoints.cs`), worst case is ~$1.50/account, but realistically far less since most accounts won't hit the cap. |
| **University research pipeline** ("Generate University" button) | Claude (now Sonnet 5, was Opus 5) + the `web_search` server tool, up to 3 fields/click, `MaxUses: 3` searches per field. | This is the actual spike you felt. Web search itself is cheap (~$0.01/search), but each search result gets fed back into context as a large tool-result block, and Opus 5's adaptive thinking (billed as output tokens at $25/1M) compounds fast on a multi-turn tool-use conversation. **This is almost certainly what exhausted the trial credit balance twice this session**, not document verification — it's a manually-triggered, bursty, multi-tool-call operation, the most expensive single action in the app by a wide margin. Already mitigated once (coverage-aware skipping, 3-field cap, Sonnet 5 downgrade, `Effort: Medium`) — this is an *operational* action you trigger by clicking a button, not a per-user cost, which matters for the framing below (§4). |
| **Hosting — Azure Postgres Flexible Server** | Burstable B1ms, West US. | Roughly $12–$25/month at that tier regardless of usage — fixed, not variable with users yet. |
| **Hosting — Azure Blob Storage** | Document originals, 15-min SAS-URI reads. | Negligible at current document volume (cents/month) — becomes a real line item only once you're storing thousands of documents, not tens. |
| **Email — Resend** | Result emails, magic links, message notifications. | Free tier covers low volume; not yet a real cost driver. |
| **Frontend hosting** | Vercel hobby tier (planned per `Plan.md` §8). | Free at current traffic. |
| **Advertising** | Not started. | §9's own plan is deliberately organic/zero-budget until message is validated — this is the one line item you haven't actually incurred yet, which is good: it means the "spending a lot of money" feeling right now is **100% AI + hosting**, not yet compounded by paid acquisition. That's the cheapest possible moment to fix the unit economics. |

**The one-sentence diagnosis**: your fixed costs (hosting) are small and stable; your variable cost is concentrated almost entirely in one manually-triggered admin action (University research), not in per-user document verification, which is the thing that actually needs to eventually be paid for by a user or an employer. Those are two different cost problems with two different fixes — don't let the University-research spike make document verification look scarier than it is.

## 2. What comparable platforms actually charge for, and who pays

### 2.1 Study-abroad platforms (closest to NOVIRA's University path)

| Platform | Model | Who pays | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[ApplyBoard](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/applyboard)** (Canada, $3.2B valuation) | Free to students. Revenue from partner institutions — a fee/commission tied to enrolled students, plus a SaaS subscription for the agent/school management platform partner agents pay to use, and advance commissions (e.g. CAN$1,200 per 3 tuition deposits) to recruitment agents. | **Institutions + agents**, never the student directly. | The purest "B2B placement fee" model in this space — but see §2.5: this is what it became, not how it started. |
| **[Leap Scholar](https://yourstory.com/2022/02/edtech-startup-leap-scholar-scaling-business-strategy)** (India, ~$6,000 Cr valuation per some reports) | Two monetized pillars: (1) counseling/application services students pay for directly, plus commissions from partner universities on successful placements; (2) study-abroad **loan/financing facilitation** — a second, independent revenue stream. | **Students** (service fee) + **universities** (commission) + **lenders** (loan-referral commission). | The financing angle is the most transferable idea to NOVIRA long-term. |
| **[WeMakeScholars](https://www.zaubacorp.com/company/WEMAKESCHOLARS-EDTECH-PRIVATE-LIMITED/U74900TG2015PTC100548)** (India, started under India's Digital India incubation program) | Scholarship search is **entirely free** to students. Revenue comes from **education-loan facilitation commissions** paid by banks (SBI, Bank of Baroda, then private banks/NBFCs) for loans originated through the platform. | **Lenders**, not students. | The cleanest "free consumer product, B2B-adjacent revenue" model in the set — but again, see §2.5 for how long it ran with *no* revenue model at all before this existed. |
| **[Yocket](https://viewfromabridge.org/tag/yocket/)** (India) | Started as a free student community; monetizes via value-add services to students and is trying to build a university-commission stream, with mixed success reported (aggregator-vs-agent-aggregator tension). | Students (some) + attempted institution commissions. | Useful as a **cautionary** data point — a large free community is hard to convert to paid without a clear wedge. |

### 2.2 India → Germany labor migration specifically (the corridor the founder asked to focus on)

- **["Employer Pays Principle" is the norm, not the exception, for skilled-worker *employment* placement.** Ethical recruitment agencies (e.g. [Hire and Care](https://www.hireandcare.de/), [DeFa](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/recruiting-nursing-professionals-from-abroad-with-defa)) explicitly charge **zero fees to the candidate** — the German employer/hospital pays. This is B2B territory (§2.2 original research) — not the near-term plan (§4), but still the eventual ceiling once B2B is viable.
- **[Triple Win](https://www.giz.de/en/newsroom/stories/triple-win-nurses-germany)** (GIZ + Bundesagentur für Arbeit): German employers pay **€7,900 gross to GIZ** per placement, with **total all-in cost per placement landing around €15,000–30,000**.
- **[TalentOrbit](https://www.talentorbit.de/en/hiring-nurses-from-abroad/)**: same **€15,000–30,000 per skilled worker**, confirming this isn't a government-subsidized outlier.
- **General German recruitment-agency fees**: **15–30% of first-year gross salary** is the standard market range for employer-paid placement.
- **Germany's own labor-shortage numbers**: an estimated **300,000 foreign skilled workers needed per year**, India's skilled-worker visa quota expanded from 20,000 to 90,000 in October 2024 (350% increase) under the India–Germany MMPA.

**Note on how this section is used now**: this is real, useful evidence for what B2B is eventually worth (§4's "later" phase) — it is *not* evidence for what to charge an individual user today, since none of these operators charge the candidate. §2.3 below is the more directly relevant research for the near-term plan.

### 2.3 Direct-to-user pricing for "help me apply" style services (added 2026-09-11, second research pass)

This is the section that actually grounds §4's recommended fee — real prices real companies charge the *student/candidate directly* for guidance/application help, as distinct from the B2B placement fees above.

- **Plain visa/application consulting help** (the narrow "help me get my application/visa paperwork right" service, no placement guarantee): **€50–300 per application**, depending on complexity — a real, currently-active market rate.
- **A full Ausbildung-to-Germany bundle**, the closest real direct comp to what NOVIRA is describing (India-focused, Ausbildung-specific, covering German-language prep + eligibility + document help + job search support — e.g. Plan Beta and similar Kerala/India-based operators in this exact space): one such provider charges **roughly ₹2,50,000 (≈ €2,700–2,800) total, paid in installments**, against a real €900–1,300/month stipend once the candidate is placed and working in Germany. This is a *bundled, high-touch* package (language classes included, not just application help), so it's a ceiling reference, not a starting price.
- **Comprehensive study-abroad counseling generally** (non-Germany-specific, Western market): $5,000–7,000 for full-service packages, $65–150/hour for point consultations, $199 for an AI-assisted premium tier (KapAdvisor) — shows the range runs from near-free (AI-assisted, low-touch) to very expensive (full-service, high-touch human counseling), with NOVIRA's existing product (AI matching + document review + light human review) sitting closer to the low-touch end of that range.

**Read**: there's a real, legitimate market for charging the candidate directly for *guidance and application help* (as opposed to placement itself, which stays employer-paid). The going rate spans roughly €50 at the narrow end to €2,800+ at the fully-bundled end — wide enough that NOVIRA's break-even framing (§4) should sit well below the middle of that range, not try to match a full-service competitor's price.

### 2.4 The scam-agency landscape (context, not a revenue idea)

Confirms what `Plan.md` §5 already documented (the €1,500–9,000 fake-job-contract fees, €6,000 fake-exam schemes): the scam pattern is consistently "ask the candidate for money before anything real has happened" — upfront visa/processing/documentation fees, pressure to pay before a real employer contact is verifiable. Every legitimate operator in §2.2/§2.3 does the opposite: either the employer pays (placement), or the candidate pays a modest, transparent, disclosed fee **for a real, bounded service delivered up front** (guidance/prep), never a vague promise of a job/visa outcome. This distinction — *paying for a bounded service* vs. *paying for a promised outcome* — is the one to keep NOVIRA's fee on the right side of.

### 2.5 How the comparable platforms actually *started* (added 2026-09-11, prompted directly by the founder's question — this is the section that most directly answers "should I focus on users first, not B2B")

The profiles in §2.1 describe what these companies eventually became — large, B2B-funded, venture-backed. None of them started that way, and the founding stories are a closer match to where NOVIRA is right now than the current-state profiles are:

- **[ApplyBoard](https://www.applyboard.com/our-story)** — founded 2015 by three brothers (Massi, Meti, Martin Basiri) who had personally lived the problem as international students themselves. They launched with **a simple online matching tool and manual, concierge-style help**, assisting a small number of students by hand in year one — not a scaled platform, not B2B deals, not investor-scale infrastructure. The university-commission/B2B model came only **after** they had proven the matching worked and had real volume to offer institutions. B2B was the reward for building a working, trusted, user-proven product first — not the starting point.
- **[WeMakeScholars](https://yourstory.com/2015/11/wemakescholars)** — founded by two former international students (Damini Mahajan, Arjun Krishna) who started by **running free Facebook groups sharing scholarship information**, which grew to 150,000+ members with **no revenue model at all** for a real stretch of time, before the company (and its eventual bank-commission revenue) existed. Audience and trust came first, monetization came only once there was something real to monetize.
- **Yocket** — same shape: began as a free student community, and its current struggle (§2.1) to convert that community into B2B/commission revenue is itself a useful cautionary data point, not just a success story to copy.

**The pattern across all three, and it directly validates the founder's instinct**: build the real thing (matching, trust, a real user base) manually and mostly-free first; let a modest, bounded, direct fee fund the operation while that happens; only pursue institutional/B2B revenue once there's a proven, real pipeline of users to offer them. NOVIRA is currently earlier-stage than any of these companies were when they started charging anyone anything — which is exactly why §4 recommends a small, break-even, user-paid fee rather than jumping to B2B now.

## 4. Current recommendation — fifth revision, 2026-09-27 (read this section first)

**This is the current, decided design.** §4.1–§4.4 as of this revision are canonical — read them fresh each time rather than trusting a number quoted anywhere else in this file or in `CLAUDE.md`/`Plan.md`/`Architecture.md`, all of which now point here instead of restating prices, specifically because this section has already been revised five times and will likely be revised again. §4.6 summarizes what earlier revisions said and why each was superseded, kept for the record rather than deleted.

**The founder's own framing, unchanged since 2026-09-11**: focus on users, not B2B, for now. Build a large user database and refine the app in Egypt, funded by fees from users who are actually ready to act. Move to B2B only once genuinely ready for it. What changed on 2026-09-26: the free/paid split now uses a **visible-then-blurred reveal** instead of showing everything free, the tiers were restructured into three (not one bundle), and Tier 3 (applying on the user's behalf) is being built and offered now, not deferred until the company exists (§4.2). What changed on 2026-09-27: Tier 2's price and scope moved to target a different, more price-sensitive segment, and Tier 3's fee split changed to properly price the real labor-risk asymmetry unique to that tier — see the reasoning under each below, not just the numbers.

### 4.1 The decided tier structure

University and Ausbildung are treated identically — one set of prices, not two separate per-path purchases.

1. **Free.** Assessment and matching stay completely free. On `/matches`, only **one** real match is shown in full (the highest-scoring one, since results are already sorted — no extra logic needed); the rest are blurred as skeleton placeholders. **Security requirement, not just a UI choice**: the backend must never send the real data for the blurred matches, only a count — the blur has to happen server-side (send one real match + a total count of the rest) so a user inspecting network traffic directly can't see what the UI is hiding. Render that count as skeleton cards in a handful of varied shapes (not one repeated template) so the blurred list still reads as real listings. **Exception**: if a user's total real match count is 1 or fewer, there's nothing meaningful to paywall, so it's shown free outright.
2. **Tier 1 — "Unlock all matches" — €30.** Removes the blur (see every real match) plus a human review of the user's profile. Priced low deliberately to convert on volume — real conversion at this price is a hypothesis to test via a real Payment Link, not a guaranteed outcome. Keep the "human review" here lightweight/templated, not a real consultation — if this tier does convert at real volume, an unbounded per-user review time cost would undermine the whole point of pricing it low. **Open build item, flagged 2026-09-27**: this review needs to actually be triggered/tracked/delivered somehow — the in-app Messages system is the likely delivery channel, but the request/tracking side isn't designed yet.
3. **Tier 2 — "Application Support" — €150** (revised down from €300 on 2026-09-27). Everything in Tier 1, plus: an AI-assisted CV/résumé and cover letter *generated* for each specific application (not just reviewed — a real scope increase from earlier drafts of this plan, see the open build item below), advice on how to actually apply, and **one structured interview** (replacing the earlier "a few human consultations"). Still explicitly guidance/preparation — the user submits their own application. No Vollmacht/RDG exposure at this tier, and correspondingly **no formal refund policy** — the founder's reasoning: there's no comparable legal exposure here to mitigate with a refund guarantee the way there is at Tier 3.
   - **Why the price and scope moved together, not just the price**: dropping to one interview (from several open-ended consultations) is real, deliberate scope reduction — less founder time per sale — which is what actually justifies the lower price, not just a discount for its own sake. This is a genuine segmentation move, not a discount on the same product: Tier 2 now targets a different, more price-sensitive buyer than Tier 3 does — someone who wants real help but can't or won't stretch to Tier 3's commitment — rather than being a cheaper version of the same self-selected buyer Tier 3 targets.
   - **The interview doubles as a natural moment to assess and pitch Tier 3**, for users where it's genuinely a good fit — a legitimate, common practice, not a bait-and-switch, as long as the interview itself delivers real value regardless of whether the user upgrades.
   - **Open build item, flagged 2026-09-27**: actually *generating* a CV (not just reviewing one someone already has) is a real, bigger feature than what was previously scoped ("review what they have, defer the generator") — likely needs its own AI-assisted drafting step plus human review before delivery. Not designed yet, tracked here so the scope change doesn't quietly get treated as already-solved.
4. **Tier 3 — "Full service" (includes applying on the user's behalf) — €1,500 total (€500 upfront + €1,000 once a suitable opportunity is found and secured)** (revised from €1,300/€300+€1,000 on 2026-09-27). Everything in Tier 1 + 2, plus actually submitting the application under the user's signed authorization. **Not self-checkout** — a real conversation (with the founder or a team member) walks the user through the agreement before they sign; payment and signature happen during/after that call, not via an instant "buy" button the way Tier 1/2 can be.
   - **Why the upfront went up specifically**: Tier 3 carries a real risk Tier 1/2 don't — the founder can do substantial real work on a case (the interview, document prep, active searching) and still not find the user a matching opportunity, since that depends on real market conditions, not just effort. The upfront has to be large enough to cover that labor regardless of outcome — and specifically large enough that Tier 3's guaranteed portion is worth at least as much as Tier 2's full price, so the harder, riskier tier never nets *less* than the easier one when a case doesn't succeed. This is the same logic retained executive-search firms use (a portion paid and kept regardless of placement, the rest on success), not an arbitrary number.
   - **Why the total is now above the real-world anchor (§4.5's €1,200 informal-fixer figure), a deliberate choice, not an oversight**: that €1,200 was one unregulated individual's own price in a market with "many fixers, many different prices" — not a stable rate to undercut. The actual competitive basis is legitimacy, not being the cheapest option in a black-market: properly registered, insured, operating in the open, with real tax/compliance costs a black-market operator doesn't carry — a real cost-plus justification on top of the trust premium.
   - **Credit carries upward**: a user who already paid into Tier 1 (€30) or Tier 2 (€150) has that amount subtracted from the €500 upfront if they upgrade — e.g. someone already on Tier 2 pays €350 more upfront (not €500), for a total of €1,350 across both purchases; someone going straight from Tier 1 to Tier 3 pays €470 upfront. The lifetime total is always €1,500 regardless of the path taken to get there.
   - **Refund policy: Tier 3 only**, unchanged from the previous revision — this is the one tier carrying real legal/trust exposure, so a refund guarantee is a direct trust-building tool for exactly that risk. **Open detail, flagged 2026-09-27, to settle when drafting the actual agreement, not now**: whether the €500 upfront is fully refundable at any point, or only before real work has started on the case — the founder's own "keep at least what I've earned" reasoning suggests the latter fits better than an unconditional refund, but this hasn't been decided.
   - One open note carried over, not resolved: whether EU/German consumer withdrawal-right rules apply to a customer in Egypt is genuinely uncertain and worth a real lawyer question eventually.
5. **On a Tier 3 refund**: the user's access drops back to Tier 1 level (matches stay unlocked), not all the way to the free blurred view — Tier 1 is the effective floor once any payment has been made.

### 4.3 Traffic and conversion tracking

The founder correctly flagged that these prices (and whether Ausbildung/University should ever diverge, whether Tier 1's €30 is right, etc.) will need revisiting based on real data as traffic grows — this needs two different things, not one tool:

- **General traffic/funnel behavior** (visits, drop-off before reaching matches) — cheap, near-zero-effort: `Plan.md` §8 already plans to host the frontend on **Vercel**; **Vercel Analytics** gives this for free once deployed there, no code changes needed.
- **Tier-specific conversion data** (how many saw the blur, how many unlocked Tier 1, how many upgraded to Tier 2/3, real revenue per tier) — this isn't generic web analytics, it's business events tied to NOVIRA's own database. This is exactly what logging each step (blur shown → unlock clicked → paid → tier) into the same order/request-tracking model already needed for the tiers themselves (§4.6) naturally provides — build that model with this in mind from the start rather than bolting tracking on afterward.

### 4.4 Open questions, deliberately not resolved yet

Build-scoped items already called out inline in §4.1 (the profile-review delivery mechanism for Tier 1, the CV-generation system for Tier 2, the Tier 3 refund-timing detail) aren't repeated here — this list is everything else still open:

A real match list can run into the dozens (29 Ausbildung matches was a real observed case this session). - **Real per-application AI cost** for the document-check step, once built — should be small, mostly reuses the existing document-verification call.
- **Whether a second country becomes worth pursuing** — revisit once Egypt data exists, not before.
- **Whether Fintiba/Expatrio (or similar) run an affiliate/referral program for blocked-account signups** — not confirmed by research, low priority.

### 4.5 The evidence: a real, founder-sourced anecdote (2026-09-14)

Unlike §1–§3 above (secondary research — public sources, competitor pricing pages, industry reports), this is **primary, first-hand evidence** — the founder personally met two real people who lived the exact journey NOVIRA is trying to serve, which makes it stronger signal than another web search, not weaker for being a sample of two.

**The story**: two 22-year-old Syrian men, now in Hannover, studying at a German university. Neither had more than a high-school certificate from Syria and a B1 German certificate from an institute that isn't well-known (not Goethe). They had **€13,000** in a blocked account — the real financial bar to even attempt this path, consistent with the ~€900,000–1,100,000 EGP first-year cost figure already cited in §2.2/§2.3. To actually get admitted and through the process, each paid a man based in Hannover **€1,200 total — €600 upfront, €600 on approval** — for full, hands-on help with the application and getting them approved.

**What this confirms**:
1. **Real willingness to pay far more than a micro-fee, once someone is a serious, resourced applicant** — not the €25–75 or €5–8 figures §4.1/§4.2 (below) proposed, which were calibrated for the average visitor, not the actual buyer at this point in the funnel.
2. **The informal-fixer economy this evidence describes is exactly the fraud-adjacent, unregulated market `Plan.md` §5/§12 already documents** — cash, no consumer protection, one person with total control over both money and outcome, and no consistent price (this exact fixer's €1,200 was one individual's own number, not a market rate — see §4.1's Tier 3 reasoning for why that changes what "competitive" actually means here). NOVIRA's whole differentiation story (transparent pricing, real identity, no vague outcome promises, properly registered and insured) is a direct, legitimate alternative to precisely this — see §4.1 for the current prices this is anchored to.
3. **The fixer's shape (full handling + a fee contingent on "approval") is legally a different thing than what Tier 2 is scoped to do** — that's acting as someone's agent toward an outcome, which is exactly `Legal.md` §1/§3's Vollmacht/RDG risk. NOVIRA's Tier 2 stays on the "we help you prepare, you submit it yourself" side of that line on purpose; Tier 3 (§4's decided structure) is where the fixer's actual shape and price point apply — see `Legal.md` §3 for the current decision on how that's being handled.

## 4.6 History: the earlier revisions, kept for the record

**Revision 2 (2026-09-11, same day as the original B2B-first draft was reconsidered)**: proposed charging **€25–75 per single opportunity** helped with, built and shipped as a real per-listing Stripe-Payment-Link CTA on `/matches` on 2026-09-12 (see `CLAUDE.md`'s "Paid application-help offer — v1" for that build's full record). Correctly established the framing that's still true today — focus on users not B2B, guidance/prep not on-behalf submission, ship the real offer before building infrastructure behind it — none of that changed. What made it superseded: the price was calibrated against what an *average visitor* could afford, using average Egyptian income as the yardstick. It also worked out the "leakage" question (users applying without paying, since `/matches` showed full details for free) by concluding that couldn't and shouldn't be prevented by hiding information — the fee had to sell labor/reassurance instead, not access. That framing is *also* superseded now, not because it was wrong, but because revision 4 solved it more directly: blurring all but one match makes "see your full list" itself a sellable thing, so the fee no longer has to carry that argument alone.

**Revision 3 (2026-09-14)**: raised the price to **€300 per path** (University and Ausbildung priced and sold separately) after the founder brought the Hannover evidence (§4.5) — direct, first-hand proof that the real buyer at this point in the funnel is a self-selected, better-resourced group, not the average visitor. This is the revision that introduced the Hannover story as the pricing anchor (still true, see §4.5) and first proposed a €300+€1,000 Tier 3 structure — the split changed again in revision 5, but the *shape* (a bounded upfront + a larger success fee) has held since this revision.

**Revision 4 (2026-09-26)**: replaced the per-path split with the current unified structure — one free match + blur, then Tier 1/2/3, and the decision to build Tier 3 now rather than wait for the company (§4.2). Locked in at **€30 / €300 / €1,300 (€300+€1,000)**. Superseded the same day it might read like it should have been final, because real reasoning about who each tier actually serves (§4.1's segmentation logic) hadn't been applied yet — see revision 5 (§4 above) for why Tier 2 came down and Tier 3's upfront went up.

The per-listing CTA code built under revision 2 has not yet been rebuilt to match the current design — see `CLAUDE.md`'s note on this.
